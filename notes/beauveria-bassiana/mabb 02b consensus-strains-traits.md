# **Pr1/Pr2 proteases, chitinases, hydrophobins, and stress tolerance** jointly shape _B. bassiana_ virulence across delivery contexts

_Beauveria bassiana_ virulence depends on a coordinated suite of strain traits — cuticle-degrading enzymes, surface adhesion proteins, and environmental stress tolerance — that collectively determine infection success after conidia are acquired from formulation surfaces or carrier media [Dhawan2020](https://consensus.app/papers/deciphering-the-relationships-among-enzymatic-systems-dhawan-joshi/a7956da7a6a25013a10bd7494b9ee3d0/)[Kolanchi2025](https://consensus.app/papers/bioprocess-and-genetic-advances-enhancing-beauveria-kolanchi-saminathan/0d6733aa5f765583982ceb952deadedb/).

## Cuticle-Degrading Enzymes: Pr1/Pr2 Proteases and Chitinases

Subtilisin-like proteases (Pr1 family) and trypsin-like proteases (Pr2) degrade the proteinaceous matrix of the insect cuticle and are among the most consistently validated virulence determinants. Overexpression of the Pr1A homolog CDEP1 in _B. bassiana_ reduced median lethal time (LT₅₀) by 12.5% compared to wild type [Fang2009](https://consensus.app/papers/expressing-a-fusion-protein-with-protease-and-chitinase-fang-feng/59ae19f6dbf55c66afdcdc2caa617739/). Sirtuin deletion mutants (ΔBbHst3, ΔBbHst4) showed reduced virulence coinciding with downregulation of multiple Pr1 proteases — Pr1A, Pr1C, Pr1F, Pr1G — and cuticle-degrading proteases CDEP1 and CDEP2 [Cai2024](https://consensus.app/papers/two-sirtuin-proteins-hst3-and-hst4-modulate-asexual-cai-tian/8b5942d8abe65e1795ab58e65f0a930b/). A mycovirus (BbPmV4-2) enhanced virulence through upregulation of seven cuticle-degrading genes including pr1B1 (eight-fold) and pr1B2, both established virulence factors [Shi2025](https://consensus.app/papers/a-mycovirus-enhances-fitness-of-an-insect-pathogenic-shi-yang/4ed5666e130b590abf0ee0439766501e/). Pr1 protease activity correlated with virulence across _B. bassiana_ isolates tested against _Dendroctonus valens_, where the most virulent isolate (Bb1801) also showed the highest Pr1 production [Zhang2011](https://consensus.app/papers/evaluation-of-beauveria-bassiana-hyphomycetes-isolates-zhang-liu/cb1420c4b473534b841264295ccd40b0/).

Chitinases act synergistically with proteases to breach the chitin-rich endocuticle. Overexpression of the endochitinase Bbchit1 significantly lowered both LC₅₀ and LT₅₀ in aphid bioassays [Fang2005](https://consensus.app/papers/cloning-of-beauveria-bassiana-chitinase-gene-bbchit1-and-fang-leng/92a0324a2bcc5b2aa4458315edc706a3/). Fusing Bbchit1 to a chitin-binding domain from _Bombyx mori_ reduced time-to-death by 23% versus wild type, outperforming Bbchit1 overexpression alone [Fan2006](https://consensus.app/papers/increased-insect-virulence-in-beauveria-bassiana-strains-fan-fang/2d49c865cfa75d9ba78a301b107085ed/). A Pr1A–chitinase fusion protein (CDEP1:Bbchit1) cut LC₅₀ by 60.5% — more than double the reduction from Bbchit1 alone (28.5%) — demonstrating that combined protease–chitinase activity degrades cuticle more efficiently than either enzyme alone [Fang2009](https://consensus.app/papers/expressing-a-fusion-protein-with-protease-and-chitinase-fang-feng/59ae19f6dbf55c66afdcdc2caa617739/). Exogenous recombinant chitinase (rChit37) added to conidia at 300 µg/mL increased efficacy against _Diatraea saccharalis_ from 60% to 96.7% mortality [Lovera2020](https://consensus.app/papers/enhanced-virulence-of-beauveria-bassiana-against-lovera-belaich/ef0b4637171b5cb39ddd13303261fab2/).

Pr2 protease activity, together with exochitinase and lipase, was positively related to virulence across _B. bassiana_ isolates, with the most virulent isolate (TV) producing the highest enzyme levels [Seyed-Talebi2020](https://consensus.app/papers/variable-induction-of-cuticledegrading-enzymes-of-seyed-talebi-safavi/ac7791506c7950e4b7d3650e7949b9f6/). However, in vitro Pr1/Pr2 activity alone was insufficient for rapid virulence screening of strains against _Tenebrio molitor_, as toxin production (beauvericin) also correlated with survival time [Cito2016](https://consensus.app/papers/cuticle%E2%80%90degrading-proteases-and-toxins-as-virulence-cito-barzanti/999d1838320b58cfac4b859547f32afe/).

## Hydrophobins, Adhesion, and Surface Interactions

Hydrophobins modulate conidial surface hydrophobicity and adhesion to the insect cuticle — the critical first step before penetration. The hydrophobin gene _hyd1_ modulates surface hydrophobicity, adhesion, and virulence; its knock-out inhibits conidial hydrophobicity and reduces pathogenicity [Wang2021](https://consensus.app/papers/the-toxins-of-beauveria-bassiana-and-the-strategies-to-wang-peng/365e9538fbee5c718e6a4896189684b1/). Mycovirus BbPmV4-2 infection increased conidial hydrophobicity and adhesion, which correlated with enhanced cuticle penetration and a hypervirulent phenotype [Shi2025](https://consensus.app/papers/a-mycovirus-enhances-fitness-of-an-insect-pathogenic-shi-yang/4ed5666e130b590abf0ee0439766501e/). The hypervirulent strain ILB308 showed higher expression of cuticle adhesion genes compared to a hypovirulent strain when grown on insect cuticular hydrocarbons [Sessa2023](https://consensus.app/papers/beauveria-bassiana-transcriptomics-reveal-sessa-oberti/4cb3ca03bbf059c5a785aa61658a4376/). A secreted carbohydrate-binding protein (BbCBM9_1), identified as a candidate virulence factor in ILB308, improved conidial germination and virulence when overexpressed in a low-virulence strain [Oberti2025](https://consensus.app/papers/from-omics-to-enhanced-fungal-virulence-overexpression-of-oberti-sessa/cfe93402deb758128082f6ec62190e55/).

These adhesion-related traits are particularly relevant when conidia are delivered from oil-based or protein-coated surfaces, where initial contact with the cuticle depends on surface hydrophobicity and binding affinity [Kolanchi2025](https://consensus.app/papers/bioprocess-and-genetic-advances-enhancing-beauveria-kolanchi-saminathan/0d6733aa5f765583982ceb952deadedb/).

## Thermotolerance, UV Tolerance, and Environmental Robustness

Stress tolerance traits determine whether conidia survive on trap, bait, or sprayed surfaces long enough to initiate infection. UV-induced mutagenesis of _B. bassiana_ ARSEF2860 yielded hypervirulent mutants with simultaneously higher protease, chitinase, and cellulase activities alongside elevated tolerance to UV, osmotic, and oxidative stresses [Sun2023](https://consensus.app/papers/uvinduced-mutagenesis-of-beauveria-bassiana-hypocreales-sun-zhang/7260b51f001053e58f808299a30ec70c/). Among field isolates tested against _D. valens_, UV tolerance and Pr1 protease production co-occurred in the most virulent isolates [Zhang2011](https://consensus.app/papers/evaluation-of-beauveria-bassiana-hyphomycetes-isolates-zhang-liu/cb1420c4b473534b841264295ccd40b0/). Mycovirus BbPmV4-2 infection conferred increased tolerance to both UV irradiation and elevated temperatures while simultaneously enhancing virulence [Shi2025](https://consensus.app/papers/a-mycovirus-enhances-fitness-of-an-insect-pathogenic-shi-yang/4ed5666e130b590abf0ee0439766501e/).

Deletion of the DnaJ homologs _scj1_ and _djp1_ impaired conidial thermotolerance, UV-B tolerance, and cell wall integrity, while increasing LT₅₀ by 26% and 38%, respectively, demonstrating that stress-tolerance machinery is functionally coupled to virulence [Hu2026](https://consensus.app/papers/differential-roles-of-scj1-and-djp1-two-dnaj-homologs-in-hu-liu/4b42d319c49a566fb9707371fb396a04/). Sirtuin deletions (ΔBbHst3, ΔBbHst4) reduced conidial production by ~50%, decreased tolerance to osmotic and oxidative stress, and lowered virulence by ~20% [Cai2024](https://consensus.app/papers/two-sirtuin-proteins-hst3-and-hst4-modulate-asexual-cai-tian/8b5942d8abe65e1795ab58e65f0a930b/).

- **UV tolerance** co-segregates with virulence in UV-mutagenized strains and field isolates selected for biocontrol [Sun2023](https://consensus.app/papers/uvinduced-mutagenesis-of-beauveria-bassiana-hypocreales-sun-zhang/7260b51f001053e58f808299a30ec70c/)[Zhang2011](https://consensus.app/papers/evaluation-of-beauveria-bassiana-hyphomycetes-isolates-zhang-liu/cb1420c4b473534b841264295ccd40b0/).
- **Thermotolerance** is linked to virulence through shared regulatory proteins (Scj1, Djp1, Hst3/Hst4) that govern both stress response and pathogenicity [Hu2026](https://consensus.app/papers/differential-roles-of-scj1-and-djp1-two-dnaj-homologs-in-hu-liu/4b42d319c49a566fb9707371fb396a04/)[Cai2024](https://consensus.app/papers/two-sirtuin-proteins-hst3-and-hst4-modulate-asexual-cai-tian/8b5942d8abe65e1795ab58e65f0a930b/).
- **Oil-based formulations** and encapsulation systems improve spore stability and persistence, indirectly preserving the expression of these virulence traits under field conditions [Kolanchi2025](https://consensus.app/papers/bioprocess-and-genetic-advances-enhancing-beauveria-kolanchi-saminathan/0d6733aa5f765583982ceb952deadedb/).

## Strain-Specific and Host-Dependent Variation

Virulence trait expression is strain-dependent and modulated by host cuticle composition. The hypervirulent strain ILB308 harbored more candidate virulence proteins, effectors, small secreted proteins, and biosynthetic gene clusters than less virulent strains, and growth on the epicuticular hydrocarbon n-pentadecane upregulated oxidoreductase genes associated with alkane degradation [Oberti2025](https://consensus.app/papers/novel-genomic-features-in-entomopathogenic-fungus-oberti-sessa/2623ab7ea00a54eb9d418a4bd4423ee7/). The hypovirulent strain, by contrast, showed delayed and alternative pathogenicity-related metabolism with higher expression of chitinases, proteases, and CYPs but slower infection progression [Sessa2023](https://consensus.app/papers/beauveria-bassiana-transcriptomics-reveal-sessa-oberti/4cb3ca03bbf059c5a785aa61658a4376/). Host cuticle chemistry substantially affected enzyme induction: the most sensitive host (_Eurygaster integriceps_) elicited the highest Pr2, exochitinase, and lipase levels, while the most resistant (_Zophobas morio_) elicited the lowest and resisted cuticular penetration entirely [Seyed-Talebi2020](https://consensus.app/papers/variable-induction-of-cuticledegrading-enzymes-of-seyed-talebi-safavi/ac7791506c7950e4b7d3650e7949b9f6/). Genetic diversity across 17 isolates revealed positively selected genes enriched in two-component signaling and toxin-producing NRPS pathways, suggesting that natural variation in these loci underpins virulence plasticity [Zhang2020](https://consensus.app/papers/influence-of-genetic-diversity-of-seventeen-beauveria-zhang-lu/7d88b0b10561528abc7a811566ed34ae/).

These findings indicate that no single trait reliably predicts virulence across all delivery contexts; rather, the combination of protease–chitinase activity, adhesion capacity, and stress tolerance — modulated by strain genotype and host cuticle composition — determines infection outcomes after conidia are acquired from oil, fluid, protein, or trap/bait surfaces.
