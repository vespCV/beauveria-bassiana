Isolation of B. bassiana
The advancement of efficacious B. bassiana formulations commences with the retrieval of promising isolates from their natural habitats, encompassing soils, insect cadavers, and even existing as symptomless endophytes within plant tissues (Fig. 1a). A principle standard method for detecting entomopathogenic fungi in soil has relied on the insect-baiting methodologies employing the larvae of Galleria mellonella [7], a practice that consistently yields isolates marked by high virulence.
Infection biology and mode of action
The infection biology of B. bassiana represents a highly coordinated sequence of events involving conidial adhesion, germination, appressorial differentiation, enzymatic penetration, immune evasion, and systemic colonization of the insect host (Fig. 1, Fig. 2). Nevertheless, their molecular mechanisms regarding host invasion, immune suppression, and toxin production warrant careful attention, as in the struggle between two competing organisms, one invariably evolves to surpass the other.
