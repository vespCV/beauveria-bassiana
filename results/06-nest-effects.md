# 6 Effect of fungi on Vespidae nests

Research question 6: is infection enough to cause colony collaps or weaken the colony so current damage falls and fewer queens and drones are produced next year.

Rose1999: colonies remove sick individuals, isolate dead, and can compensate with more brood; capped cells may shelter pupae. A contaminated forager is a step on the path; nest collapse is a separate endpoint.

## Documented colony-level outcomes

| Taxon | Treatment | Scale | Colony / caste outcome | Cite |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| *P. dominula* | Naturalis, 1 μL of 10⁶ spores/μL topical | lab / colony boxes | colony failure; 80.77% exposed larvae ejected; fewer eggs; smaller ovaries | Cappa2024 |
| *M. metathoracicus* | Boveril label rate | individuals | LT50 19 d; CHC overlap; nestmates treated alike; authors flag a possible colony threat | DeSouza2023 |
| *P. dominula* / *V. germanica* | Eco-Bb 2 g L⁻¹ nest spray ± EPN | field nests | mix infected 31.39% ± 4.75% larvae and 3.42% ± 0.68% pupae; adult emergence similar across treatments | VanZyl2024 |
| *P. chinensis* | *B. malawiensis* oral 2.68 × 10⁶ cfu mL⁻¹ | three treatment nests | higher adult mortality vs controls; ~3.3% natural infections in the population | Reason2022 |
| *A. mellifera* hives | GHA or Bb05002 in wax powder, two doses | field hives | mite fall up with Bb05002 + carnauba; colony weight, adult mass and brood similar to control | Meikle2008 |
| *V. velutina* apiaries | 0.01% fipronil protein bait | 90 questionnaires | ≥2-week drop at high pressure; pupal hatch as rebound source | Barandika2023 |
| *Vespula* | Vespex 0.1% fipronil | 300 × 50 m bait grids | 93–97% traffic reduction | Edwards2017 |
| *V. maculifrons* | 0.5% fipronil catch-treat-release | field colonies | secondary mortality in untreated recipients | Buczkowski2024 |
| *Vespula* spp. | protein bait + *Metarhizium* or Bb | field baits | nest traffic reduced (*Metarhizium*); infected larvae from both fungi | Brownbridge2009 |

VanZyl2024: mean 25–29% of larvae still pupated after water, EPN, Bb or mix; pupae-to-adult percentages overlapped (p = 0.464).

Small numbers of gynes are enough to found next year (FeasSanchez2019).

Santos2026: bee pathogens present in queens as well as workers, larvae and pupae (possible vertical path). Villemant2015: mermithid nematodes in French *V. velutina*. Kim2025: *Xenos* strepsipterans on *V. velutina* workers in South Korea. Kosaka2026: *Sphaerularia vespae* sterilizes overwintered queen hornets in native *Vespa* spp. Felden2020: mite-associated fungi and viruses in diseased *Vespula* nests. Yang2026: *Serratia* associated with *Vespa analis* larval death.
