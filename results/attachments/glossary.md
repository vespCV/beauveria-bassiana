# Glossary

Plain-language explanations for terms that appear in this review but sit outside normal beekeeping practice (hive management, Varroa control, feeding, queen rearing). Strain codes and product names follow the papers; EU authorisation status is on the to-do list for the Netherlands.

## The hornet and the goal

| Term | Explanation |
|------|-------------|
| Yellow-legged hornet (*Vespa velutina*) | The invasive hornet that hunts honey bees at hive entrances. Dutch name: geelpoothoornaar. This review asks whether a fungus can weaken the hornet colony, not only kill individual hunters at the apiary. |
| Colony weakening (project endpoint) | Less hornet pressure this season and/or fewer queens and drones that would start nests next year. Comparable to caring about mite levels and winter bee quality, not only whether one bee dies on the landing board. |
| Colony collapse | The nest fails entirely: brood and workers die off and the colony does not recover. Stronger than weakening, rarely demonstrated for Bb in hornets. |
| Foundress | A mated queen that starts a new hornet nest in spring. Poidatz2019 isolated Bb from a foundress that was already infected in the wild. |
| Apiary pressure | How many hornets show up at hives (Barandika2023 counts hornets in front of hives). Protein bait trials report pressure going down for at least two weeks after treatment. |

## The fungus and how it kills insects

| Term | Explanation |
|------|-------------|
| Beauveria bassiana (Bb) | A soil-dwelling fungus that infects insects. Many commercial biopesticides use it. Strains differ in how well they kill and survive in sun and dry air. |
| Entomopathogenic fungus (EPF) | Any fungus used (or studied) to kill insects. Bb is one EPF. Papers also mention *Metarhizium*, *Beauveria malawiensis*, and others. |
| Conidia (spores) | The microscopic infective units. You can think of them as fungal "seeds" that stick to the cuticle, germinate, and grow into the insect. Products are sold by spore count (e.g. per gram or per millilitre). |
| Cuticle | The insect's outer shell. The fungus must breach it to reach the body cavity. That step needs moisture and suitable temperature. |
| Mycosis | Infection by a fungus. Papers report "mycosis rate" after exposure (e.g. sawfly trials in soil cages). |
| Sporulation | After the insect dies, the fungus may grow out of the body and produce new spores on the cadaver. That is how infection might spread to nestmates that touch the body. |
| Germination | The spore starts growing. Hive temperature matters: Peng2020ao reports that about 35 °C inside a honey-bee hive stopped germination of formulated Bb spores, while 25 °C still allowed high forager mortality in their assay (abstract-only, PDF requested). |

## Delivery: traps, baits, and the nest pathway

| Term | Explanation |
|------|-------------|
| Catch-infect-release | A selective trap or bait station lets the target hornet in, she picks up spores from bait or a contact surface, and she leaves and flies home. The hope is she carries inoculum into the nest. Same idea as poisoned protein baits used in hornet campaigns, but with fungus instead of insecticide. |
| Selective trap / bait station | A device or bait point aimed at hornets rather than at spraying the whole field. "Selective" means the design favours the pest picking up the agent. |
| 1-way and 2-way dispenser | Hive-mounted passages used in bumblebee trials: the bee walks through powder or spores on the way out (1-way) or in both directions (2-way). Evidence that spores can load onto hairy pollinators. Hornet-specific dispensers are still open. |
| Sugar bait vs protein bait | Hornets take both at different times of season. Merino2007 used sugar bait with spores. Barandika2023 and many hornet campaigns use protein bait (often fish-based) with a biocide. |
| Horizontal transmission / nestmate transfer | Spread inside the nest by contact: a returning forager touches nestmates, brood, or comb material and passes spores before hygiene behaviour removes her or the cadaver. |
| Autodissemination | Insects spread spores to each other without a human spraying every individual. Demonstrated in beetles and bed bugs. Social wasps are the relevant model for hornet nests. |

## Social insects: nests, hygiene, and recognition

| Term | Explanation |
|------|-------------|
| Open-comb nest (*Polistes*, *Mischocyttarus*) | Brood cells open to the air. Spores can land directly on larvae. Photo in README §2.2. |
| Enveloped nest (*V. velutina*) | Brood sits inside a paper shell. Spores from a trap mostly enter with returning workers rather than by direct spray onto larvae. |
| Nestmate recognition | Wasps and bees identify nestmates by surface chemicals (CHCs). Bb exposure can change those profiles and how aggressively nestmates react (DeSouza2023, DeFazi2025). |
| CHC (cuticular hydrocarbons) | Waxy surface chemicals on the exoskeleton. Guard bees and wasps use them to accept or reject incoming individuals. |
| Hygienic removal | Colonies throw out sick or dead nestmates and may isolate brood. Rose1999 and VanZyl2024 discuss this as a barrier to nest-level kill from a single contaminated forager. |
| Capped cells | Cells sealed over pupae (like honey-bee brood caps). Pupae in wasp trials were often less infected than open larvae after nest spray (VanZyl2024). |

## Lab language you will see in the papers

| Term | Explanation |
|------|-------------|
| LD50 | Lethal dose: amount of substance (often per insect body weight) that kills half the test group. Common for chemical baits. |
| LC50 | Lethal concentration: spore or chemical concentration that kills half the group. Example unit: spores per millilitre (10⁷, 10⁸). |
| LT50 | Lethal time: days (or hours) until half the exposed insects die at a fixed dose. Poidatz2018: about 6.25 days for half the hornets at ~10⁷ spores/mL. README often writes this as "half died in X days" instead of "LT50". |
| Lab bioassay | Controlled test in cups or cages: known spore dose, temperature, and humidity. Shows whether the fungus *can* kill. Field trials show whether it *does* under rain, sun, and real nests. |
| Field trial / in-nest spray | Test on real or relocated nests outdoors or in situ. VanZyl2024 sprayed nest surfaces. Infection rates were lower than in lab larval tests. |
| Proxy | Evidence from another species (bumblebee dispenser, *Polistes*, palm weevil trap) because direct *V. velutina* + Bb data are scarce. Always labelled in the review. |
| Abstract-only (`ao` citekey suffix) | The citekey ends in `ao` (abstract-only), e.g. Peng2020ao, Almeida2022ao. The repo holds the abstract and metadata only. The full PDF is missing and listed for acquisition in [`input-phase2/pdfs_request_list.md`](../../input-phase2/pdfs_request_list.md). Treat numbers and outcomes as provisional until the PDF is filed under `pdfs/`. README may also tag `abstract` on citekeys without the `ao` suffix when the same applies. |
| [project inference] | A conclusion drawn across several papers, not a single quoted result (e.g. one contaminated forager alone is unlikely to collapse a nest). |

## Products, strains, and regulation

| Term | Explanation |
|------|-------------|
| Strain | A specific genetic line of Bb (e.g. GHA, ATCC 74040). Strains differ in virulence and heat tolerance. "Bb" on a label is not interchangeable without data. |
| GHA, ATCC 74040, R444, ESALQ PL63 | Commercial or research strain codes in the papers. BotaniGard and Velifer use GHA. Naturalis uses ATCC 74040. Eco-Bb uses R444. Boveril uses ESALQ PL63. |
| EU active-substance approval | The fungus strain is approved at EU level as a pesticide active ingredient (six Bb strains in this review). That is step one. |
| National product authorisation (Netherlands: Ctgb) | A specific product must be authorised for a specific use on the Dutch market. EU strain approval alone does not tell you whether a trap or bait use is legal in NL. That remains **[to be verified]** in this project. |
| Formulation | How spores are packaged: wettable powder (WP), emulsifiable concentrate (ES), oil, granules, etc. Oils and waxes help spores survive dry air and UV (Mascarin2016). |
| Non-target | Organisms you did not intend to treat (honey bees, bumblebees, native wasps). Bee papers split between "safety test" and "pathogenicity test" framings. Both appear in README §2.3. |

## Other abbreviations in the evidence

| Term | Explanation |
|------|-------------|
| EPN | Entomopathogenic nematode. VanZyl2024 combined nematodes with Bb. The mixture gave higher larval infection than Bb alone in that trial. |
| USDA-ARS | US public agricultural research agency. Author affiliation on several proxy papers. |
| Citekey (e.g. Poidatz2018, Peng2020ao) | Short label linking a sentence to one paper. Suffix **`ao`** = abstract-only in this repo, full PDF requested. Full references sit in the phase-2 extract tables and Zotero library. |
