# Beauveria bassiana systematic review

Systematic review of *Beauveria bassiana* (Bb) for use in selective traps, baits, and autodissemination devices (catch-infect-release) aimed at reducing the impact of *Vespa velutina* (Vvel) (yellow-legged hornet) in regions where it is an invasive species. Focus is on contaminating or loading hornets with Bb, enabling horizontal transfer within nests, and assessing outcomes from mere exposure through to actual infection and colony impact.

## The idea story

A *Vespa velutina* (yellow-legged hornet) enters a selective trap. There she can drink sugar bait or take protein bait carrying *Beauveria bassiana* conidia, or pick up conidia from the 2-way dispenser as she passes (comparable with pollinator vectoring). She flies back to the nest with the spores. Nestmates (adults, pupae, larvae) may pick them up by contact (horizontal transfer). On the cuticle, hyphal tubes grow from the conidia, through the skin into the body cavity. That can kill the hornet, change her behaviour, or fail if she clears the infection. In a dead insect the fungus can grow out and form new conidia. The endpoint is colony collapse or colony weakening: less current damage, and fewer queens and drones for the next year.

## Research questions

1. Which Bb strains are commercially available in the EU, what formulations exist (companies), and what use is allowed in the Netherlands?
2. What are the chances that Bb can kill or weaken Vvel and/or non-target species in the field (not only in a Petri dish or plastic container, but in the nest)?
3. How can we deliver Bb so Vvel can take the conidia with her (formulation, dispenser)?
4. Can she transfer conidia to nestmates (larvae, pupae and adults), what concentrations/CFU are needed on the hornet, and can an infected insect still enter the nest?
5. Are circumstances in the nest sufficient for the fungus to grow (temperature/humidity)?
6. Is that sufficient to weaken the colony so fewer queens and drones are produced next year, or even to kill the colony?

## Background

### What is Bb and how does it do its job?

- Bb is in the soil all over the world
- Bb has a lot of strains each with own strengths and powers
    - separate Botanigard batches differed in ethanol tolerance, pathogenicity, colony growth and spore production (Moore2026)
    - Recultured BotaniGard killed more pheretimoid earthworms than label-as-prescribed product; 1-year refrigerated spores showed little mortality (Nouri-Aiin2021).

**Infection follows seven steps** (Valero-Jimenez2016)
1. host adhesion
2. germination
3. cuticle degradation
3. growth as blastospores
4. host colonization and killing 
5. immune-response interactions
6. hyphal extrusion
7. conidiation 

Efficacy is limited by humidity, temperature, rainfall and UV (Mascarin2016).

## 2 Effect on Vvel --> vespidae --> other hymenoptera --> other insects

Spray or contact results on distant proxies do not stand in for *V. velutina*.

**V. velutina.** Poidatz2019 describes a *B. bassiana* strain from a naturally infected foundress in Bretagne: morphology, molecular ID, and mycelium growth versus temperature (fastest growth 20–28 °C; optimum about 20–22.6 °C). Full-text check: Poidatz2019 reports no hornet bioassay mortality, LT50 or LC50. Poidatz2018 bioassays the same foundress isolate BB on adult hornets at about 10⁷ spores/mL (direct inoculation, contact, transfer, food): direct inoculation gave the highest mortality index (0.60; food 0.14, transfer 0.26, contact 0.35; Table 1 pooled across fungal isolates); LT50 for isolate B was 6.25 ± 0.67 days (mean 5.8 ± 0.44 days across isolates); LC50 is absent from Poidatz2018. Likely natural uptake was during hibernal diapause in soil or wood, or during wood-pulp collection (Poidatz2019). Application above 20 °C was recommended for field use (Poidatz2019). Formulation, timing, and risk to prey (bees) remain open in the discussion (Poidatz2019). First-author funding: Bayer Crop Science (Poidatz2019). A later hornet/wasp/bumblebee comparison used *Metarhizium robertsii*, not Bb (Lacombrade2025; first author at M2i). Bee and bumblebee pathogens occurred across larvae, pupae, adults and queens in 38 *V. velutina* nests; that is a shared nest compartment, not a Bb assay (Santos2026). Other *V. velutina* papers in the notes (protein bait with fipronil, viruses, nest architecture, nematodes) do not measure Bb.

**Vespidae.** Liquid sugar bait at 1 × 10⁸ conidia mL⁻¹ with Chilean isolates Qu-B941 and Qu-B933 killed 79% and 95% of *Vespula germanica* workers and 66% and 73% of males in the lab; 1 × 10⁸ conidia mL⁻¹ raised worker mortality to 90% and 97%. Those isolates are not commercial EU products (Merino2007). Lab inoculation with Eco-Bb (R444) killed all tested *V. germanica* and *Polistes dominula* larvae by day 7. In situ nest spray (2 g commercial product per litre) is inundative nest treatment, not catch-infect-release. The EPF/EPN mixture infected 31.39% ± 4.75% of larvae and 3.42% ± 0.68% of pupae; pupae were largely spared (VanZyl2024). Boveril (ESALQ PL63) on *Mischocyttarus metathoracicus*: LT50 19 days; slower than imidacloprid (DeSouza2023). Naturalis (ATCC 74040) on *P. dominula*: higher mortality, altered locomotion and feeding, 80.77% of exposed larvae removed (41 of 52), reduced oviposition and ovary development in foundresses, colony failure. Topical dose 1 μL of 10⁶ spores/μL (Cappa2024).

**Other Hymenoptera.** Honey bees: ICIPE 284 contact mortality did not exceed 17.4% under hive-simulated conditions; stingless bee *Meliponula ferruginea* stayed at or below 11.0% (Omuse2022). Lab contact and ingestion of commercial Bb reduced survival of *Apis mellifera* and *Bombus terrestris* at the individual level; less hairy bees may pick up more conidia; oral exposure can exceed topical (Leite2022). Isolate NI8 killed 98.2% of honey bees at the highest lab concentration at 10 d (Portilla2017). Hive-mounted bumblebee dispensers put detectable Bb on 97-99% of *Bombus impatiens* workers; some workers infected; no epizootic and no adverse impact on colony health, brood or activity (Al-Mazraawi2006). Safety-framed bee papers often report little Bb effect; control-framed papers often report mortality.

Apple sawfly (*Hoplocampa testudinea*): soil-applied BotaniGard GHA gave 49-68% lab mycosis but only 17% in field soil cages; GHA persisted above background through the larval-descent window, then reverted within a year (Swiergiel2016; hymenopteran soil proxy).

**Other insects.** High spray or contact mortality is common in aphids, whiteflies, beetles, flies, thrips and moths (Wraight2010; Parker2015). Those are proxies. Local *B. bassiana* isolate Bb-01 on house flies: 96% adult mortality at 3 × 10⁸ spores/mL by immersion; bait LC50 7.42 × 10⁶ spores/mL; sublethal doses cut fecundity (Farooq2016). Rice weevil (*Sitophilus oryzae*): soil Bb isolates at 2.11 × 10⁷-10⁸ conidia/mL gave 0-100% adult mortality in 14 d, highest when conidia were applied directly on adults (Kavallieratos2014). Poultry-house manure treatment with GHA or 707 (granular corn best): 60-90% lesser mealworm/hide-beetle larval suppression at 2 weeks (Geden2003). Colorado potato beetle: soil Bb isolates LT50 11-17 d; fastest strain in the panel was *M. robertsii* MAN3b at 5.0 d (Konopicka2024). Autodissemination is shown in sap beetles, kissing bugs and bed bugs (Dowd2003; Forlani2011; Barbarin2012). Field failure also occurs: three *B. bassiana* applications did not reduce spotted lanternfly numbers (Keller2023).

## 3 Mechanics and delivery

Two pickup modes sit side by side: contact on a passage surface (1- or 2-way dispenser), and inoculum in sugar or protein bait. Neither is established as the winner for Vespidae.

Hive-mounted 1- and 2-way dispensers work for bumblebees vectoring Bb onto crops (Al-Mazraawi2006; Kapongo2025). Maize flour carried more conidia through bee dispensers than coarser meals; pass time did not (Al-Mazraawi2007). Hairy pollinators and bee-vectoring are delivery-system evidence, not Vespidae proof. NZ protein baits with *Metarhizium* and Bb reduced *Vespula* nest traffic; infected larvae recovered from both fungal treatments (Brownbridge2009). Red-palm-weevil pheromone traps with Broadband gave LT50 4.15 d at 10⁸ spores/mL (Hajjar2015; weevil proxy). EAB autocontamination and autodissemination devices (Lyons2012; Srei2020) and medfly autoinoculation with sterile males (Toledo2017) are beetle and fly proxies.

Oil formulations improve infection at low humidity, thermal stress tolerance and survival under solar radiation (Mascarin2016). HEC and alginate hydrogels kept Bb viable up to 24 days in a mosquito ovitrap assay, longer than liquid suspension (Friuli2025). Carnauba wax in dry powder raised virulence in a blowfly assay (Muniz2020). Corn-oil coating improved granular thermotolerance (Kim2010). Grasshopper bait mycosis tracked surface contamination during feeding more than ingestion (Inglis1996). Dry Bb conidia repelled *Odontotermes formosanus* workers in a dose-dependent manner; longifolene at 0.2-0.4 mmol mL⁻¹ reversed that in bait (Lin2026; termite proxy). Poultry-manure granular corn bait outperformed EC and residue formulations for lesser mealworm in lab confirmation after field trials (Geden2003). House-fly bait and immersion both load Bb-01 conidia; bait LC50 7.42 × 10⁶ spores/mL (Farooq2016). MCD bottle bioassay adapts WHO-style exposure to slow-acting Bb powder on malaria vectors (Sternberg2014; mosquito proxy). Whether dry conidia repel *V. velutina* is not filled.

UV is a field constraint. After simulated sunlight, Ignoffo and Garcia (1992) reported a half-life of *B. bassiana* conidia of about 2 hours (Zimmermann2007). Transgenic *B. bassiana* expressing archaeal CPD photolyase survived 4-7 h natural sunlight far better than wild type and kept virulence on *Anopheles gambiae* after exposure that removed kill by irradiated wild-type spores (Fang2012; engineered mosquito proxy).

## 4 Horizontal spread in the Vvel nest

Autoinoculators can move a marked Bb strain into sap-beetle overwintering sites (Dowd2003). Horizontal transmission of powder formulation in *Triatoma infestans* scaled with bug density (Forlani2011). Catch-treat-release with fipronil shows the social-wasp design pattern without Bb (Buczkowski2024). Protein bait with 0.01% fipronil reduced *V. velutina* pressure at apiaries for at least two weeks in one trial (Barandika2023; not Bb).

Nestmate response to Bb-exposed social wasps splits by species:

- *Mischocyttarus metathoracicus*: CHC profiles similar 24 h after Boveril; wasps did not discriminate exposed from unexposed conspecifics (DeSouza2023).
- *Polistes dominula*: Bb altered the CHC profile and increased aggression toward exposed nestmates and foreign individuals, without breaking nestmate recognition. Higher aggression is interpreted as keeping infected individuals out of the nest (DeFazi2025).
- Stingless-bee proxy: *Tetragonisca* guards exclude pathogen-exposed nestmates (Almeida2022ao).
- Ants differ in susceptibility and autogrooming; concentrations about six orders of magnitude above natural levels did not give 100% mortality (Bos2019).

Santos2026 found bee pathogens in all sampled *V. velutina* stages, including possible vertical transmission from the queen. That supports a shared nest compartment. It does not measure Bb transfer, CFU on returning foragers, or whether an infected hornet is still admitted.

Required load (CFU per returning forager) for Vvel nest introduction is not filled.

## 5 Fungal growth on the Vvel

Germination on the cuticle and sporulation after death both need high moisture (Zimmermann2007). Optimum temperature for *B. bassiana* is about 23-28 °C, minimum about 5-10 °C, maximum about 30-38 °C, isolate-dependent (Zimmermann2007). The Poidatz foundress isolate was described as better adapted to intermediate temperatures; application above 20 °C was recommended for installation and dispersion (Poidatz2019). Fu2026 isolate WZS5 (melon fly proxy) kept activity at 35 °C and transferred at 30 °C (LT50 5.28 d at 1.0 × 10⁸ conidia mL⁻¹; LC50 1.32 × 10⁷).

Seven hours of natural sunlight killed about 60% of photolyase-deficient *Metarhizium* spores; archaeal CPD photolyase in *B. bassiana* raised spore survival after 4-7 h sunlight up to 44-fold and preserved virulence on *Anopheles gambiae* (Fang2012; engineered proxy).

In honey-bee hives, in-hive temperature near 35 °C abolished germination of formulated conidia while forager mortality was high at 25 °C (Peng2020ao). That is honey-bee nest climate, not *V. velutina*. Nest temperature and relative humidity of *V. velutina* are not filled. Nest paper of *P. dominula* is hydrophobic (VanZyl2024).

## 6 Effect of fungi on Vespidae nests

Nest failure from disease has seldom been recorded. Colonies remove sick individuals, isolate dead, and can compensate with more brood; capped cells may shelter pupae (Rose1999). A contaminated forager is not nest collapse.

Documented colony-level Bb outcomes in social wasps:

- *P. dominula* in lab/colony boxes: colony failure after topical Naturalis, with brood ejection and reduced foundress reproduction (Cappa2024).
- *M. metathoracicus*: delayed individual death (LT50 19 d) plus no avoidance; the authors flag a possible threat to colony survival, not a measured nest kill in the field (DeSouza2023).
- *P. dominula* / *V. germanica* field nests: inundative spray, mixture better than Bb alone, pupae little infected (VanZyl2024).
- *P. chinensis*: oral *Beauveria malawiensis* at 2.68 × 10⁶ cfu mL⁻¹ raised adult mortality in three treatment nests (Reason2022).

Open for catch-infect-release in *V. velutina*: whether a selective trap loads enough conidia onto a returning worker to infect adults, larvae and pupae and reduce queens and drones; whether an EU commercial strain is enough, or a Vespidae isolate is needed; whether formulation (oil, UV protection) and timing above 20 °C make the path reliable; whether nest T/RH allows outgrowth.
